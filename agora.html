The Living Agora · Ancient Intellectual Life

Meet the thinkers where they spoke

Enter into conversation with figures of the ancient Aegean world — grounded in what actually survives of them. Each answer marks its own footing: a surviving fragment, a modern reconstruction, or an interpretation. The first to open the gate is Heraclitus of Ephesus.

Reconstruction of the Athenian Agora · illustrative

The distinction that matters: these are not modern chatbots wearing ancient costume.

Heraclitus below speaks only from what ancient authors actually preserved of him — a small, curated set of fragments, each with the writer who quoted it and a public-domain translation. When he grounds a point in his own words, he cites the fragment, and you can open it in the panel to the side. When he reasons past the evidence, he says so and marks it: reconstruction, later reception, or speculation. If your question runs past the surviving text, he will tell you that plainly rather than invent a source.

This is Phase 2 of the project: one figure, text only. The grounding is the point.
